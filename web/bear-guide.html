Keep troops hitting through the whole Bear.

Your own rallyThe rally you start. This march is extra to your joins.

A joinA march you send to someone else’s rally.

1 Use every joining march

Split your troops equally

Use all your available joining marches. Open Optimize Me and split your troops between joins using its exact counts.

Save the troop mix and first hero it gives you. Your own rally has a separate troop setup.

The troop cap is a limit, not a target you must fill.

2 First available, every time

Join the next rally to hit

Keep rally favorites off. The next rally to leave should be at the top of the list. Join the first one you can use.

Skip your own rally, full rallies, and any you cannot reach before they leave. Send all your joins as soon as there is room.

Have to wait a minute for space? That is fine.

3 Everyone starts a rally

Start with your group

Everyone needs to start a rally so we have enough places to join. Start when your wave starts, using your own-rally setup.

A small delay is fine. Aim to keep the groups about one minute apart, rather than starting everyone at once.

Keep your own-rally troops and heroes ready for your start.

4 Through the whole Bear

Get home. Go again.

As soon as a joining march gets home, send it out again. Always join the first available rally. Do not wait for your other marches.

As soon as your own rally returns, start it again. Your wave time sets your first start; after that, keep going.

A march is ready? Use it. No space yet? Try the next rally that opens.

When Bear starts

  1. Wave 1 players: start your own rally immediately. Select Bear, tap preset 5 and start. Do this before sending joins so everyone has rallies to join.
  2. All players: open the first available rally. Tap preset 1 and join. Use preset 2 for the next rally, then preset 3 for the next.
  3. Use preset 4 again and again for every joining march you still have free. One saved preset can send several separate marches.
  4. Later waves: when your wave starts, select Bear, tap preset 5 and start your rally. Then keep sending joins.

When a march returns, use its preset and send it again. Joins always take the first available rally. If there is no room, wait for the next one to open.

Waves, without the confusion

A wave is a group of rally starters

Groups start one minute apart. This gives returning troops more rallies to join.

Your group tells you when to start. It does not limit who you join.

You do not need all your joining marches to fit in one wave. Troops hit, return, then join another rally.

Why this works

More small hits can beat one big hit

The plan is about your total damage over the whole Bear.

Why take the first available rally?

Do not park troops in a whale rally

A whale is a very strong player. Their rally can give you a big hit. But joining it with 4 minutes left locks those troops there while other rallies hit.

Example: the same 40,000 troops score 1 million on a normal rally, or 3 million on the whale. These are made-up numbers to show the trade-off.

The same 40,000 troops, used again after each return
What you doHitsTotal damage
Wait in the whale rally1 × 3 million3 million
Keep joining: 4 hits4 × 1 million4 million
Keep joining: 5 hits5 × 1 million5 million
Keep joining: 6 hits6 × 1 million6 million

Five hits beat the whale by 2 million. Six beat it by 3 million. Even four hits win in this example.

Five or six hits in four minutes needs a full hit-and-return cycle every 48 or 40 seconds, including time to join. That needs enough rallies leaving and short trips. One usable departure per minute will not give you five or six hits.

Join the whale when it is the next available rally.

Use your battle reports: one whale hit only wins if it beats the damage from all the other hits you could make while waiting.

Over the whole Bear, taking the first available rally should give everyone a few chances to join our strongest leaders. Who gets in will vary with timing. The aim is to keep troops moving and help the whole alliance score more together.

Filling the whale early also blocks other players

If the whale rally fills early, players who follow the first-available rule may find no space when their troops return. They end up waiting or taking a weaker rally, even though they followed the plan.

Keeping those strong-rally spaces open until they are needed helps make the plan fair for everyone.

Why split troops?

Twice the troops does not mean twice the damage

This is called diminishing returns: adding more troops to one march gives a smaller boost each time. Sharing them across marches can give you more total damage.

All 40,000 in one hit1.00×Our starting point
20,000 in each of two hits1.41×About 41% more in total
10,000 in each of four hits2.00×About twice the total

This uses the optimizer’s square-root model, with the same troop mix, tiers, rally strength and number of hits per march. It is an estimate, not a promised score. Each example uses 40,000 troops in total.

The tool splits your troops evenly and works out the mix for you. Use every joining march you have. More marches only help if you keep sending them.

Why these heroes?

The right skill helps the whole rally

When you join, only your first hero’s first Expedition skill can add a rally bonus. The rally can use up to four joiner skills. Your other heroes do not add extra rally skills.

Chenko · Yeonwoo · Amadeus

Boost Lethality: your troops hit harder. Each gives up to 25% at skill level 5.

Amane

Boosts Attack: another way to raise damage. She gives up to 25% at skill level 5.

Matching bonuses add together. These two types of bonus multiply together, so a mix can be stronger.

Four Lethality joiner skills1 + 0.25 + 0.25 + 0.25 + 0.25 = 2.00×
Two Lethality + two Attack skills1.50 × 1.50 = 2.25×

A simple level-5 skill example: 2.25 is 12.5% more than 2.00. This compares those joiner bonuses with other effects held fixed, not your whole event score.

Follow the first-hero choices in your setup. If it says No hero, send without heroes. Your troops still hit, and you avoid adding an unwanted skill. Your own rally uses all three leader heroes’ Expedition skills.

Two quick checks

A few troops left over?

Equal joining presets can leave a few troops after rounding. Add them to your own rally in game if it has room. Keep your joining presets the same.

Bear is nearly over?

The rally must hit before Bear ends. Leave time for its timer and the trip to Bear. With a 5-minute timer, start before 25:00 and allow extra time for travel.

For our strongest rally leaders

Keep your rally spaces useful

Your strong rally helps the alliance most when players can join it at the right time. Keep the same rhythm: start with your wave, then restart as soon as your own march returns.

If players join your fresh rally right away, remove them if you have time.

Do this during the countdown, while several minutes are still left. Their troops can return and join a rally that will hit sooner. Your spaces stay open for troops returning when your rally is next to leave.

  1. Start your rally. Use your wave time for the first one.
  2. Check for very early joins. If someone joins straight away and you have time, remove them from the gathering rally.
  3. Keep the rally moving. Leave players who are joining when your rally is next available. Restart promptly when your march returns.

Checking early joiners is optional. Keep sending your own joins and restarting your rally; those come first. You are freeing spaces, not asking people to skip your rally.

If your rally fills early, players who follow the first-available rule may find no space when their troops return. Keeping those spaces open helps make sure players benefit from following the shared plan.
