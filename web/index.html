What the simulator does

The browser engine, validated against the Python reference, tracks a separate host slot, every joining slot, rally troop capacity, joiner count, travel, impact and troop return. Troops and slots are reserved when sent and cannot be reused until they return. One member may join a given rally once. All policies share the same roster and mechanics.

Expected contribution = troops × troop quality × host strength × common score scale

At the default exponent, damage is linear in troop count. The exponent control changes the troops term to troops × (troops / 50,000)^(exponent − 1) for sensitivity testing. The model estimates expected damage; random hero procs and detailed battle rounds are not simulated.

In equal-split mode, selected hosts divide troops across joining slots plus their own rally. For your 226,505-troop example that is about 37,751 per allocation when hosting, or 45,301 across five joining marches when joining only. Concentrated mode fills marches to capacity and can exhaust troops before all slots are used.

Host selection ranks the empirical host-strength input, breaking ties by rally capacity. Power and town level help identify a player but are not used as damage multipliers or automatic eligibility thresholds. You can uncheck Host or use the explicit host names in exported project JSON to test particular leaders.

Efficient joining compares host strength and time until impact plus return per committed troop. It considers open rallies only; it does not predict unavailable future rallies or solve every assignment jointly. Staggered hosts retain their own phase and cycle; travel variation can change the actual spacing. Reaction delay applies between a member's successive joins and after a host cycle.

14 joiners per rally and capacity including the host remain editable starting assumptions. Confirm the joiner cap in your game. The owner confirmed that only hits during the event score; late hits are excluded. Join travel is a per-player estimate; a future version can accept a city-to-city travel matrix.

How to calibrate

  1. Record actual member attendance, usable troops, march capacity, rally capacity and travel.
  2. Use several comparable contribution reports under each host to estimate damage per equivalent troop.
  3. Enter your actual existing schedule as the custom strategy, or as normalization.strategy in project JSON, and validate against event totals and individual scores.
  4. Compare policies using a fixed damage scale. Repeat with a different host-strength spread and damage exponent to check whether a recommendation holds.
  5. Trial the schedule in one event and replace assumptions with observed results.

Source boundary

The rally countdown, separate host slot, travel range and full-event score range come from your description. Community guides support the distinction between host stats and joining troops, but their scheduling advice varies. No verified public combat formula is claimed.
